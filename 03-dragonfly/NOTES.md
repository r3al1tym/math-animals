# 03 Emperor dragonfly (`03-dragonfly`)

Between its main veins a dragonfly's wing is divided into small cells. Hoffmann, Donoughe, Li, Salcedo and Rycroft (PNAS, 2018) measured those cells in the wings of 232 odonate species and proposed a model that reproduces them: inhibitory centres spaced evenly inside each region bounded by the primary veins, with the secondary veins set where the centres' signals are weakest. That puts each wall midway between two centres, so the cells form a Voronoi tessellation: each is the part of the wing nearer its centre than any other. The model is a proposal; the signal itself has not been found. The page builds the emperor's fixed frame first: forewing 50 mm and hindwing 49.8 mm, outlined in Catmull-Rom curves, with the costa, subcosta, R1, the radial sector and its branches (RP1, RP2, IR2, RP3/4), MA, MP, CuA and the anal veins, the nodus, arculus, triangle and pterostigma. Each frame is rasterised at 20 px per mm and flood-filled into its regions, 21 in the forewing and 18 in the hindwing, with a distance transform giving every point's clearance from the nearest vein.

The leading-edge fields are narrower than two cells, so their centres lie in one row down the middle of each field: between the costa, subcosta and R1 before the nodus, between the costa and R1 after it, under R1 on both sides of the nodus, and over the first stretch between RP1 and RP2. One centre is set every local spacing along the field's midline. Elsewhere the centres are scattered by dart throwing: seeded random points are kept when no centre of the same region lies nearer than a minimum spacing, and gaps are then filled around the kept ones. The spacing is a field that falls from 0.9 mm at the root to 0.5 mm at the tip, a little smaller toward the trailing margin and larger in the costal strips and the hindwing's anal field, so the cells shrink outward as the paper measures. Across the four wings, which each get their own seeds, the spacing runs from 0.44 to 1.28 mm and gives 2,611 cells, 509 of them in the single rows. Each cell is a square around its centre cut by the perpendicular bisector of every neighbour in its region, and each wall is trimmed where it meets a main vein. A single row of centres therefore gives a ladder of straight crossveins, the antenodals and postnodals, while the wider fields toward the tip and trailing margin fill with polygons. The pterostigma is one cell with no seed. Of the 623 polygon cells clear of the main veins, 15 have four sides, 192 five, 294 six, 115 seven and 7 eight.

The drawing registers with the specimen because both come from the same numbers. Every seed and wall is pencilled at natural size under the pinned wings, which ride 12 mm up the pin, and where a wing stops each open margin cell's walls run on up to 2.6 mm onto the bare card, fading. A dimension line over the wings gives the span, tip to tip. A pencil box on the right hindwing, 31.4 by 12.6 mm from just short of the nodus to past the tip, is drawn again at ×6 as Fig. 2, centred under the specimen at the wing's own angle and cropped square by its frame. The box and its cone are drawn in one graphite. The figure holds 445 seeds and 1,021 walls, a seed in every cell. The plate's axis runs down through the box and the figure, so the cone between them is symmetric and the specimen sits left of centre with its label beside it. The figure is in mid graphite so that the animal leads: the costa, R1 and the nodus heaviest, the other main veins next, the walls lighter, and every stroke at its own pressure. The build scatters the seeds in a shuffled order, lowers the specimen 54 mm onto its pin, and then grows a front at one speed from every seed. A wall pixel appears once the front has reached it, and since a wall is the same distance from both its seeds it appears where two fronts meet: the rings in Fig. 2 close into cells while the wings above fill with veins at the same instant.

The wings are glass. The membrane is nearly clear, amber only at the root, so the card and its pencil show through. The venation is near-black like the figure's walls, and each cell catches a faint cool reflection of the window a little differently, as the corrugated wing does. The wings cast their venation onto the card 12 mm below, offset 12 × MM × K along the light and blurred by about half a millimetre, at about half the body shadow's strength. In that shadow the fine walls blur to a tone, and the main veins and the pterostigma show as soft lines and a soft patch down and to the right of each wing. The pterostigma darkens from pale to brown as the build ends. The male's colours are apple-green thorax and frons, blue eyes, a sky-blue abdomen with a black dorsal line over ten segments, a yellow costa and a brown-yellow pterostigma. Body length is 77.5 mm and span 104.5 mm, both measured off the drawing.

## Trials

Four crops of the same right hindwing, each centred on its own patch, 4.4 to 6.0 mm across, shown at ×5.7 to ×7.8. In each, the seeds are laid by another rule at the wing's own density there and cut by the same Voronoi code. Each disc is the middle of a sample 2.4 times its radius. The side counts are over that sample's interior cells, not counting the vanishing edges where four cells meet at a grid corner.

- square grid, between RP3/4 and the margin: 4 sides (57 cells)
- hexagonal lattice, between MA and the trailing margin: 6 sides (30 cells)
- minimum spacing, the wing's own seeds near the tip between RP1 and RP2, ticked: 4 to 7 sides (46 cells)
