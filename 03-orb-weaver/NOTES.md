# 03 Garden orb-weaver

The web is laid on its construction drawing: 31 rays from the hub and the Archimedean spiral r = a + bθ with 2πb = 4.6 mm, drawn in white pencil as broken lines (the rays as chain lines, the spiral dashed), so the drawing reads apart from the continuous silk. Under the silk the lines are drawn light; past it they are firm. The rays run on past the frame, and the spiral runs on past the silk to the frame. In one window at the web's left, between two rays, the curator ran the rule on into open card for five more turns. On the web itself, under the silk, a scale is laid beside one radius. It is divided at every turn of the drawn spiral, its divisions filled and open in turn, from the first capture turn out into the window, so the even pitch can be checked against the silk turn by turn. A bracket across its last division is lettered 2πb = 4.6 mm, and an arc spans one gap between rays, lettered with its measured 12.2°. The radii are near-equal, as the spider measures each gap against the last: the gaps run from 10.0° to 13.3°, with a mean of 360°/31 = 11.6° (*Araneus diadematus* builds 25 to 35 radii, mean 31.5 in field webs). The silk is spun on the drawing in the spider's own order: bridge and frame, radii, a dry auxiliary spiral outward from the hub (pitch 13.5 mm), then the sticky spiral from the edge inward. The hub stands high: the capture area is an oval hung below it, so the web has 7 capture turns above the hub and 15 below. Where a turn would cross the frame the spider turns back, so the outer rows are partial loops in the lower web, joined by U-turns, as Zschokke (2011) describes for this species. She tends to turn back on the same radius for a few rows running, so the turnbacks stack into short seams down the web's flanks. She places each crossing by leg reach, so the silk wanders up to 0.5 mm off the drawn turn; between radii the capture thread runs taut and straight. The dragline silk of the bridge, frame and anchors is the heaviest thread, laid as two or three strands, the radii are next, and the capture spiral is the finest, its brightness carried by its glue beads. Each frame thread is drawn in a little by the radii fixed to it and bows toward the hub by about 1.2% of its span. Between the hub's strengthening zone and the first sticky turn is a free zone 12 mm wide, where only the drawn spiral winds. The auxiliary spiral is cut away as the sticky rows pass it and leaves a small ball of silk on each radius. Each silk chord is lit by the window as a cylinder: it sends light to the camera, 400 mm above the drawer, only where (L + V)·t = 0. The window is about 10° across, so the glint has a width. Before beading the web shows a bright streak of capture turns through the hub along one diagonal, with the lit radii on the other. The silk is a cool white and the graphite a warm grey. Off the glint a beaded turn is lit only by its beads; in the glint the bare thread lights its full width too, and the turn is two to three times as bright. The web stands 6 mm proud of the card on its anchor pins, so every thread drops a faint shadow 6 mm × K down-right.

The glue coat is a liquid cylinder, and the page finds its instability itself. Rayleigh's growth rate σ² ∝ x(1 − x²)·I₁(x)/I₀(x), with x = kr, is maximised numerically at x = 0.6970, a wavelength of 9.01 r (often quoted as 9.02). A coat of r = 16 µm (the detail's thread; the coat drifts by about 13% from row to row as the spider's glue output varies, and a thicker coat breaks into bigger, more widely spaced beads) therefore beads every 144 µm, about seven beads to the millimetre, each bead holding one wavelength of coat (radius 30 µm), with smaller satellite droplets between them and, now and then, two beads run together. Measured Araneus droplets are 28 to 67 µm long at 72 to 264 µm spacing (Opell & Hendricks 2007). At drawer scale that is two beads to a pixel, so every bead's catch of the window is splatted at its true position. A bead shows the window whichever way its thread runs, so when the coat beads, every turn lights all the way round. The bare core between the beads is still a thin cylinder, so the glint cross survives the beading: the turns run from about a quarter of full brightness away from the glint to full brightness in it. The beads are prolate, 1.3 times as long as wide, which adds a little more light where a thread lies square to the window. The ×100 detail circle shows one junction near the web's foot, ringed on the web and joined to it by tangent leaders. The dry radius, about 5 µm across, runs straight through the junction and carries no glue. The capture thread crosses it, and its glue has gathered into a drop where the two threads are glued together, so the first bead on either side sits a wavelength out. Its beads come from the same model and are shaded as clear drops: the window's highlight, a bright Fresnel rim, light gathered on the side away from the window, and the glycoprotein nodule at the centre. They grow out of the smooth coat during the moment.

The spider is an autumn female at life size, 16 mm, head down on the hub. She is a per-pixel height field (carapace and an egg-heavy abdomen with humeral humps), shaded under the window and painted with the species' dorsal pattern: a brown ground, the lobed dark folium and the cream cross of guanine spots, about 5 mm each way. Her eight legs are banded and spined, and each set of claws rests on the nearest radius. The curator lowers her onto the hub as the inner turns close. Her shadow falls twice: close under her on the silk she stands on, and 6 mm further down on the card. The web's six anchor threads are tied round stainless pins at 6 mm. The pins stand 8.5 mm proud of the card, and the camera above the drawer's centre sees each shaft lean out a little under its head. Each pin casts its shadow down-right.

## Numbers on the card

All are computed by the page: 31 radii, mean 360°/31 = 11.6°, the window's measured gap 12.2°, 2πb = 4.6 mm, ×100, r = 16 µm, 9.01 r, 144 µm. The glue-coat radius is a modelling choice inside the measured range for Araneus. The rest are literature values: radius count, web asymmetry and U-turns (Zschokke 1993, 2011; 31.5 radii and 5.6 mm mean spacing in field webs), and droplet sizes (Opell & Hendricks 2007).
