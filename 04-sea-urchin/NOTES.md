# Plate No. 4: the test of the edible sea urchin

The test of *Echinus esculentus* is a dome of twenty columns of plates in ten double meridional areas: five narrow ambulacra, pierced by pore pairs for the tube feet, alternating with five broad interambulacra. At the ambitus the page makes each ambulacrum 0.6 of an interambulacrum's width (27° against 45°): descriptions of Echinidae give ambulacra half to two-thirds as wide as the interambulacra at the ambitus, and field keys for this species note the five ambulacra as paler bands, so the test reads five-fold. Its symmetry is the dihedral group D5: five rotations through multiples of 72° and five mirrors, drawn on the card as the chain lines through each ambulacrum and the interambulacrum opposite. The page builds one sector, one ambulacrum and one interambulacrum (ambulacrum III and interambulacrum 3, in Lovén's numbering), and makes the other four as its rotations through 72°, 144°, 216° and 288°. The real test is not quite D5. The two columns of each area are offset by half a plate, so their median suture zigzags and a mirror maps a column onto its neighbour only half a plate out of step. The madreporite, the sieve plate of the water-vascular system, is genital plate 2 and occurs once, so of the five mirrors only the one through interambulacrum 2 and ambulacrum V still holds for the apical disc: the test is really bilateral. The page keeps both details.

The plate code lays each column by arc length down the meridian of a dome 140 mm across and 108 mm tall, a superellipse |ρ/R|^2.25 + |(z − 46.2)/61.6|^2.25 = 1 above the ambitus. In plan the test is round: Echinus is near-circular. The trials draw their n-sided outlines with a soft-polygon warp, 5% deep. New plates form at the apical disc, under the ocular plates, and are pushed down as the test grows, so the plates near the top are the youngest and smallest. The page models this with a plate index k = n·x^0.72, x the fraction of the arc from the disc to the ambitus, so plate height grows as x^0.28: interambulacral plates run from 2.3 mm tall at the disc to 8.9 mm at the ambitus, ambulacral compound plates from 1.4 mm to 6.4 mm. Each column has 15 interambulacral or 21 ambulacral plates above the ambitus. These counts and the exponent are the page's choices: real counts rise with a test's size and were not measured from a specimen. Each ambulacral plate is compound, of three elements with three pore pairs set in an arc, and every second or third one carries a primary tubercle. Each interambulacral plate carries one primary tubercle in a meridional series down the column, flanked by secondaries about two-thirds its size, with smaller ones above and below on the taller plates and miliary granules between. Tubercle size follows plate size: the interambulacral primaries grow from about 1.0 mm across at the disc to 2.8 mm at the ambitus, the ambulacral ones from 0.9 mm to 2.3 mm. That grading is drawn stronger than a large Echinus shows (in large specimens the primary and secondary bosses are near equal; only in small ones do the primaries stand out), so it reads at feed size. The tubercles are imperforate and smooth, as in all Echinidae: a boss in a shallow areole under a polished mamelon. The apical disc, about 24 mm across, is dicyclic: five genital plates, each with its gonopore, ring the open periproct, and five paler ocular plates, each with its pore, sit in the notches between them. The madreporite is larger, swollen and finely sieved.

The colour runs gently from violet at the apex to rose and pale toward the ambitus, and varies by area (the ambulacra paler and more lilac), by column, by plate and by a slow mottle that travels with the plates. The test is evaluated per sample on a 2× grid into fields of albedo, micro-relief, height and normal, then shaded under the window each frame, with matte Lambert light, a little wrap for the porous calcite, a darkening toward the rim with light thrown back into it from the card, the quarter turned from the window 15% darker, a rosy glow on the shadow side where light passes through the thin test, and tubercle shadows marched along the light over the height field. Because the shading happens after the fields are turned, a sector rotated to a new bearing is lit where it lies. The build draws the construction from 0.4 s: the ambitus, the five chain-line mirrors along the medians, the ten area boundaries, the apical disc and sector 0's plate sutures and zigzags; then sector 0 drawn as a 72° wedge on the card, its two edges running firm out from under the test along their sutures to the ring, its outer arc firm, sparse diagonal pencil hatching between the ring and the rim, lettered "one sector, 72°" along its own arc; and four faint arrows round the ring, each from one sector edge to the next round from the hatched sector, the four turns of the label. The mirror axes stop at the test's edge, where they meet its median seams: pencil lies on the card under the specimen, so no line crosses the test. The disc is set at 1.5 s. From 1.8 s to 3.0 s sector 0 grows down its drawn meridians: every column's plate index is shifted by end × (1 − e), so plates come in under the disc, grow as they are pushed down, and come to rest each on its drawn cell. From 3.0 s to 4.15 s four copies come out from under sector 0 like the blades of a fan, blade j turning 72·j·e degrees along its drawn arc, and the symmetry closes as the fifth sector meets the first. The shadow is the union of the test's height slices, each thrown z·K along the light and blurred by height, for only the part of the test that exists at that moment, thrown at 0.13 of the window's true length so it reaches 25-30 px past the rim, graded, and with a tight dark contact under the rim on the side away from the window. The test is dry and chalky: matte, with no specular, its albedo a fifth less saturated, its plates each a tone of their own, and each boss in the outer third throwing a small shadow down-right. The adradial sutures between the areas read strongest; the median zigzags are quiet, so the ten areas read as five broad fields and five narrow strips. On the wall at the ambitus the plan view looks down the wall for the boss that stands out furthest, so the rim tubercles break the silhouette. The pores are dark pits lit with the dome, at about 60% of their first contrast. The 50 mm scale bar sits under the specimen, and the trials and label are lettered by 4.5 s.

## Trials

The same plate code at n = 4, 5, 6 and 7, each drawn as a small urchin in plan: a rounded n-sided outline (a soft polygon 5% deep, so n shows at this size), the apical ring and periproct, in each ambulacrum two pore zones of pore pairs, one pair to each compound plate, and in each interambulacrum its two series of primary tubercles, one to each plate, all laid by the specimen's columns. They run at even gaps from the left wall to the label, their captions (n and the angle: 90°, 72° ticked, 60°, 51.4°) on the label's last two baselines, under the note "five-fold is the rule; four- and six-fold tests are rare abnormalities".
