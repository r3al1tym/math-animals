# 05 Chambered nautilus

A half shell of *Nautilus pompilius*, cut in the plane of coiling and laid cut face up at natural size (178 mm across), lies on its own construction: the equiangular spiral r = a·e^(bθ) with b = 0.175. Every turn the spiral grows by W = e^(2πb) = 3.00 and it crosses every radius at the same angle, α = arccot b = 80.1°; the page letters both. A ray runs from the pole through each septum of the last whorl (21 to 35) and out past the shell, so every ray's foot shows the venter crossing it at α. The pencil curve is laid along the shell's outer margin, so the edge reads as the curve, and runs on a quarter turn past the aperture, where rays 25 to 27 cross it at the same angle again. Septum 25's ray is measured: the septum sits 40 mm from the pole, and the curve crosses the same ray at 120 mm. Measured shells give 2.9 to 3.0 a whorl (Moseley's specimen averaged 2.94; 80 Smithsonian shells averaged 1.31 a quarter turn), so the nautilus is not the golden spiral, which grows φ⁴ = 6.85 a whorl.

The shell is the rule, sampled per pixel. A point's whorl is found by unwrapping its angle about the pole until it lies between one venter and the last, which gives its fraction q across the whorl. The 35 septa are one curve, θ = θk + g(q), its dorsal end leaning 11° forward and its middle bowing back toward the apex, placed every 25° (the mean spacing in *Nautilus*), so each is the last one scaled by e^(25°·b) = 1.079 and turned 25° about the pole; the last two crowd to 18° and 12.5°, as an adult's do. The siphuncle is a parallel spiral at 45% of the whorl, threading every septum through a septal neck. The cut face is a height field (the cut plane 40.7 mm above the card, each chamber sunk to the half-width of the whorl, the septum's concave face sloping into the chamber behind it), shaded by marching a soft shadow toward the window through the field, with occlusion and one bounce of light between the nacre walls. The open living chamber also takes light from its own far side, so its shade is soft and gathers along the inner wall, and the window's reflection lies across its floor where the floor turns 24° toward the window. The chambers are a luminous warm cream pearl: the wide window's light spreads softly down into each, and each is about 25% darker on the concave side of its septum. The pearl's colour (rose, green and blue, turned by slope and view, each chamber its own cast) shows only in a narrow band along the rim and the septa's edges, faintly. Each septum thickens where it meets the outer wall; the chamber wells fall dark, so the cut face spans the full range from shade to window light, a bright septal bead marks each septum's neck on the siphuncle, and the outer wall is cut through at about 1 mm (4.2 px): white porcelain outside, carrying the brown flame stripes in broad irregular bands round the whole exposed whorl and fading out over the last 60° to a white aperture, with nacre inside. The septa have real thickness (0.7 to 1 mm in the last whorl), thicken where they meet the outer wall and catch the light on their concave side; the faint pink-green sheen shows on the walls only. The shell sits warmer and about 20 to 35% darker than the card. Under the soft cast shadow, a deeper shade along the lower-right margin shows it standing about 3 mm clear of the card on its curving flank, and a tight contact shadow 2 to 3 px down-right seats it. The body chamber is a hollow: the inner whorl casts a crescent of shadow across its floor, it darkens where the previous whorl sits against it, fine growth lines about 3 px apart cross its floor, curved like the aperture's margin, every sixth a little stronger, and a bright 2 px lip of nacre runs along the cut wall and the aperture. Three states are baked (open tube, back wall laid, sealed) and each frame picks between them per pixel.

The build is the growth. The pencil draws the spiral out from the pole in one line, carried on a quarter turn past the aperture where the rule goes on and the animal stopped, and rules each septum's ray from the pole as the curve reaches it (the earlier septa get a tick). Then the aperture sweeps out along the drawn curve from the 5.7 mm first chamber, and a septum seals one living chamber's length (110°) behind it at each numbered point, the curator going over that ray's foot once more as it does; the living chamber opens last and the light falls into it. The trials along the foot are drawn by the same model: the same first chamber grown to the same adult, the same whorl (its dorsum a third of the way out from the pole), the same septa, and only W changed.

Label (ink): No. 1 / *Nautilus pompilius* Linnaeus, 1758 / chambered nautilus, half shell / r = a·e^(bθ), b = 0.175

Trials: W = 2 (whorls overlap, the band each whorl shares with the last hatched), W = 3.00 ticked (whorls touch), W = 5 (the coil opens), W = φ⁴ = 6.85 (the golden spiral, drawn over the squares of its golden rectangle, the curve through their corners).

Sources for the numbers: b = 0.1759 fitted to a cut shell (Wikipedia, Logarithmic spiral); Moseley's whorl ratios 2.83 to 3.02, mean 2.94, and Bartlett (2018), 80 shells, mean 1.310 per quarter turn; septal spacing about 25° and body chamber 85° to 110° (Ward et al. 1981, for *N. macromphalus*); adults 30 to 38 septa; adult diameter 170 to 180 mm in most populations.
