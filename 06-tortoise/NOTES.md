# 06 Radiated tortoise: growth by accretion

A tortoise scute never stretches. Keratin is added only at its edges, so every season's outline survives on the shell as a ring (an annulus), and the hatchling's own scute stays at the centre as the areola. The page models the shell keeping its shape as it grows, so each ring is the scute as it was that year: a copy of the areola scaled about the scute's growth centre. With a hatchling of 36 mm (radiated tortoises hatch at 32 to 40 mm) and this juvenile at 150 mm, the seam is the areola scaled 150/36 = 4.17 times, and the page gives the shell 9 annuli of equal carapace length, so ring k is the areola × (1 + 0.352 k). The growth centre sits where the real areola does, off the scute's middle: toward the tail on a vertebral, high toward the spine on a costal, on the free edge of a marginal, nuchal and supracaudal, and a little different on every scute. The yellow rays run out from that centre, laid down by the growing edge: 6 to 10 on each vertebral and costal, their width set per scute and their lengths varied, spread round the scute and kept clear of the corner tracks, and 2 or 3 broad wedges on each marginal, nuchal and supracaudal, fanning in from the free edge. Each ray tapers from the areola's edge to a point that lands on a growth line, between about two fifths and nine tenths of the way to the seam, and the growth lines, incised in the keratin, run faintly across it. The scute topology is fixed and counted: 5 vertebrals, 4 costals and 11 marginals a side, one nuchal and one undivided supracaudal, 37 scutes, every seam a three-way junction.

The pencil construction is the carapace itself, drawn so that every part of it can be seen from above. The dome is a superellipse in plan (150 × 116 mm, narrowing toward the head) with a 66 mm high profile z = 66 (1 − u^2.7)^Q, Q from 0.8 over the bridge to 0.92 fore and aft, whose wall turns smoothly out into the marginals' flare below 17 mm (up to 7 mm at the front and 6.2 mm at the rear, dipping over the supracaudal so it turns down over the tail); the free edge is notched at every marginal seam, 2.3 mm deep at the rear. It is sampled on 1440 meridians. Above 30 mm the construction is drawn in plan, exactly where the standing shell will cover it; below the 30 mm contour the flank is laid out flat beyond the fold, each meridian unrolled at true arc length on its own azimuth. So the pencil collar that runs out from under the shell is the shell's own lower flank, with all 22 marginals, their areolae on the free edge (hatched, as the hatchling's keratin), their rings and the costals' outer rings, and its seams leave the shell's edge in line with the seams on the shell. The pattern is computed once on the carapace's surface (a chart of arc length from the crown, baked at 7.2 px per mm): each sample finds its scute by a scanline fill, then its gauge (distance from the growth centre over the border's distance in that direction, from a 2048-entry polar table) gives the ring, the rays in straw to ochre keratin, the mottled yellow areola (each star's centre is the hatchling's whole scute), the near-black brown horn between the rays with a fine amber-brown line at each ring, a little lighter or darker from scute to scute, the relief (the areola a plateau 0.22 mm proud; each year a low convex band stepping down 0.11 mm toward the seam, with a groove up to 0.2 mm deep at its line, the same depth for the same year on every scute; a soft sulcus at each seam; each vertebral and costal a boss 3.6 mm high between recessed seams, each marginal 1 mm) and the gloss, satin and rubbed matte on the yellow, the crown and the marginals, whose free edges are scuffed and worn pale. The hatchling's scutes appear on their pencil areolae with their own small stars; every scute then adds its nine rings at once, the gaps between them closing until the seams meet; then the carapace stands: the part above the fold rises in place to its height, and the flank draws in from the card, its free edge sliding to the rim as the fold rises. The standing shell is ray-cast per pixel through the inverse of that map, with 2×2 samples and up to ten more along the meridian where the flank turns away, and lit by the window as satin keratin: Lambert, the room and the card's bounce, the window's four panes softly mirrored on each scute's boss, and the pale ceiling along the vertebral ridge. Its long shadow is the shell sliced at thirteen heights, each slice thrown h·K along the light and softened with height, kept light; over it lies a firm shadow about 18 px down-right of the near flank and a dark contact line where the free edge meets the card.

One costal, the second on the right, is carried out past the rim onto the card at life size, unrolled: its areola shaded solid, its nine rings at twice the construction's weight, numbered k = 0 (the areola) to 9 (the seam) along the track to the middle of its marginal seam, a dashed leader running back to the same scute on the shell, and the rule lettered beneath it.

The trials along the foot are drawn in plan from the same scutes and the same dome, with the hatchling's keratin, the areola, shaded solid in graphite. The 36 mm hatchling sits in the middle at life size, every scute all areola; to its left it is scaled up whole to 150 mm at half size, still all areola edge to edge and no rings, drawn light because it is impossible for a shell that grows only at its edges; to its right it grows by accretion at half size, a small solid areola in each scute inside its 9 rings, in full graphite and ticked.

## Modelling choices to state plainly

- The card says the rings are offsets of the areola. The page scales the areola about a growth centre instead, because real areolae sit off-centre (on the free edge of a marginal, high on a costal) and the bands are wider on the far side; equal offsets would centre every areola. Scaling follows from keratin that does not stretch on a shell that keeps its shape.
- Equal length added per ring and a shell that keeps its shape are the page's assumptions; real annuli vary with the season and are not a reliable count of years, so the label never gives an age.
- The 150 mm size, 66 mm height and 9 annuli are this specimen's, chosen for a juvenile (adults reach 26 to 40 cm).
