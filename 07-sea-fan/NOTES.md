# 07 · Common sea fan (`07-sea-fan`)

A dried common sea fan lies on the card at true size, 205 mm across and 168 mm from the foot of its holdfast to its margin, grown by space colonisation (Runions et al. 2005, 2007) with fusion. The curator pencils the fan's free space as 4,526 attractor points inside an uneven, lobed outline 216 × 165 mm, taller on the left than the right and notched between its lobes. They are Poisson-disc spaced a = 1.9 mm apart, the spacing running from 0.78 to 1.28 a across the field in patches a few centimetres wide, so the mesh is finer in some parts of the blade than others. Each attractor pulls the one branch node nearest it within 2.5 a (4.75 mm); every pulled node grows 1 mm along the mean of its pulls, so tips extend and older nodes put out side branches where space allows; an attractor is consumed when a node comes within 0.6 mm. The stem climbs from the holdfast until free space first pulls it, then divides unevenly into three main ribs, at 54° and 11° left and 32° right of the vertical (G. ventalina's stem divides close to its base). A tip left with nothing pulling it reaches on toward an unrelated branch up to 4 mm ahead and fuses where it touches (anastomosis), so the open tree closes into a planar net. Thickness follows the pipe model (r^3.6 summed over the branches a node carries, tips 0.36 mm): the stem is 3.3 mm in radius, the ribs 1.6 to 2.8 mm. The colony is collected at growth step 156, while it is still climbing into its tall left lobe: 3,839 attractors consumed, 2,624 fusions, and 1,653 enclosed openings, median 2.1 mm across, with half the open area in openings at least 3.2 mm across (the figure on the card), measured by rasterising the grown net at 0.2 mm and flooding the card the outside can reach. Tissue is 60 % of the closed blade.

The page grows the colony once at load and keeps every node's birth step, so `seek(t)` rebuilds the colony as it stood at that step. Each branch is a rod rasterised per pixel, its section a slightly flattened ellipse (exponent 2.1, height 0.8 of its half-width), each axis smoothed along itself through its branch points. The rind is chalky: the light wraps far round each branch and the shade side is lifted by the light the chalk scatters, so the branches are modelled lit side to shade side with no dark edge, with a chalky bloom on the lit side. The colour is graded by girth, from deep aubergine on the trunk and main branches (value 0.40) through plum on the ribs (0.53) to pale lilac on the finest mesh (0.76). Each branchlet dried to its own girth (each chain up to about a fifth thicker or thinner), and the thin ones carry their polyps as low, uneven mounds about a millimetre apart. The blade stands 2.8 mm proud of the card on its ribs and is cupped up to 2.6 mm, so its shadow falls about 12 px down-right. The shadow has four parts, combined by their maximum so crossing shadows never darken twice: a faint soft shade under the whole blade (12 %), the net's crisp lace (33 %), a firmer core under the ribs (42 %), and the holdfast's shadow with a tight contact shadow at its foot. The holdfast is a lumpy, pitted fragment of warm limestone about 50 × 23 mm and 6 mm thick, broken off with the colony, its pits occluded, with the crust spread over it from the stem's foot (2 mm) and a collar where the stem is seated; it props the stem up. The attractors are stippled up the field before the colony starts, and each is rubbed out to a ghost at the step a node reaches it. At the hero every attractor still free near the front is tied by a pencil hairline to the node it pulls, and three fusions made in the last 30 steps at the growing front are ringed.

The trials run the same function on the same outline and grain with the same seed for the same 156 steps, changing only a, and each shows the same 48 mm window at true size. The window is laid on the specimen itself, as a quadrat is laid on a sample: a cotton thread square pinned at its corners through the net, on a dense patch of mesh a third of the way up the blade (centre 78 mm left of the stem, 58 mm up). Two pencil leaders run from where its sides come out from under the blade down to the ticked trial, with its caption between them. The trials are pencilled at the branches' true thickness in firm, even graphite and revealed in growth order. At a = 7 mm (348 attractors) the free space is too coarse for a stalled tip to find a branch within reach: an open tree, no fusions. At 1.9 mm it is the specimen itself (2,624 fusions, ticked). At 0.8 mm (25,242 attractors) the branches crowd so close that the net all but closes: a blade, 77 % tissue, openings median 1.3 mm, 5,507 fusions. The foot runs the trials across its left two thirds and the label, with its two lines of notes, at the right.

## Notes

- The fusion rule (a stalled tip reaching up to 4 mm to an unrelated branch ahead) is this page's proposal for anastomosis inside a space-colonisation model; Runions et al. (2005) close their loops differently, by letting one attractor pull several nodes. Kaandorp's accretive growth models of corals are the related literature. The card calls it "a model".
- The girth variation, the section's flattening, the polyp mounds, the quadrat and the holdfast are rendering; the counts and opening sizes are measured on the grown model. The three-rib start and the grain field are modelling choices: they reproduce the stem's division close to the base and the uneven mesh of a real blade, and the trials share them.
- The trial windows are 48 mm (1.2 times the earlier 40 mm) and stay at true size.
- `pencilMany` lays the 4,526 attractor dots and the trials' strokes in one graphite pass each through the kit's own `_trace` and `_graphite` (as `letterText` batches its strokes); one `pencilDot` per attractor cost 4.8 s of init.
