# 10 Semicircle angelfish

A juvenile *Pomacanthus semicirculatus*, 120 mm long and laid on the card at true size, grows out of its own construction: a Turing reaction–diffusion pattern on a skin that grows by similarity (Kondo & Asai 1995). Juveniles of this species take on the adult colours somewhere between 8 and 16 cm, so 120 mm is still inside the juvenile range. Two substances react on the skin with piecewise-linear kinetics of the kind Kondo uses in his simulations. The activator u follows f = clamp(0.08u − 0.08v + 0.05, 0, 0.18) − 0.03u, the inhibitor v follows g = clamp(0.1u − 0.15, 0, 0.5) − 0.06v, and the steady state is u = 3.0, v = 2.5. The inhibitor diffuses 25 times faster than the activator (Dv/Du = 0.1875/0.0075); these kinetics need more than 10.26 to make a pattern. The activator also spreads 3 times faster along circles round the tail point than across them (α = 3, an anisotropic diffusion after Shoji, Iwasa, Mochizuki & Kondo 2002), which turns a labyrinth into the juvenile's semicircles. That anisotropy is this page's assumption; nobody has measured it in the fish.

The page solves the model once at load, in the fish's own frame. Growth by similarity only scales the diffusion by 1/s² and dilutes both substances at 2r, so the field is never resampled while the fish grows. Instead the grid is refined twice (2, 1 and 0.5 mm of the 120 mm fish), which keeps a cell between 0.33 and 0.8 mm of real skin. It steps with explicit Euler, an isotropic nine-point Laplacian and zero flux at the skin's edge; the caudal fin is left out of the domain. The 20 mm fish holds its size for 900 time units while a small excess of activator at the tail spreads forward as rings, then it grows exponentially to 120 mm, doubling every 3000. Along the midline the stripe count goes 3, 5, 7, 7, 12 and 13 at 20, 40, 60, 80, 100 and 120 mm. The old stripes stretch apart with the skin until a new stripe opens between two of them, at 20.6, 40.3, 43.8, 46.3, 50.4, 83.4, 88.2 (two), 98.6 and 104.3 mm. The mean spacing on the midline stays near 7.5 mm (8.1 mm at 30 and 40 mm, 7.3 at 60, 6.7 at 90, 7.5 at 120) while the fish grows six-fold. At 120 mm a new stripe is still opening between two old ones under the dorsal fin, and that one is ringed on the fish.

The fish is coloured per pixel from 74 saved states. The ground runs from a near-black back to a lighter navy belly, modelled by the window at the upper left, and the finish is matte. White is read in a narrow band near the activator's crest, so the stripes are crisp and their widths vary as the crests do; they thin toward the arcs' centre at the tail. The thin pale-blue lines between them are the inhibitor's valleys: v is lowest midway between two stripes, where the next one will insert. The scale lattice is about 0.95 mm apart, which gives about 67 scales along the lateral series (the species has 65 to 70); each scale's free rim is a hair lighter and drops a hairline shadow. The fins are membranes on their own. The body stands 2 mm proud of the dorsal and anal fins, lit along its upper edge and shaded along its lower, and the fins carry the dark ground and the stripes, with fine rays and margins that clear toward the edge: dorsal XIII + 21 and anal III + 20, inside the published XIII, 20–23 and III, 18–22. The rounded rear lobes come down to a narrow caudal peduncle. The 17-ray caudal fans out from it, dark at its root and clearing toward the margin. The clear pectoral fin has 19 pale rays, and the pelvic fin lies along the belly with its white leading spine. The body is 11 mm thick at its middle. The shadow is eleven height slices, a tight contact shadow of the whole silhouette at 0.35 mm and a soft one at 2.6 mm (about 10 px down-right).

The growth is drawn as Kondo and Asai showed it: the same fish at 20, 40, 60 and 80 mm, each at true size and in the fish's own outline (the solver's domain is that outline, less the caudal fin). Each is drawn from the solver's state at that length, with its white stripes shaded in graphite and its length and stripe count lettered under it. They lie in a row on one midline under the 120 mm fish, and each is drawn as the growing fish passes its length. The stripes multiply (3, 5, 7, 7) while their spacing holds. The fish itself grows in place by similarity about a point in its head and fills its pencilled 120 mm outline. The posterior stripes are arcs about the tail point. Where each meets the fin margins, the pencil carries it on round the tail across the card and through the clear caudal fin, and one spacing between two of those arcs is dimensioned (7.9 mm). Last, the new stripe under the dorsal fin is ringed in white pencil on the fish, with a graphite leader to its note.
