# Brief for the ten drawer builders

You are building one drawer of Math-Animals: one self-contained HTML page in `NN-name/index.html`. Read, in this order: `CONCEPT.md` (the series), tenfold's `README.md` sections "How they are built" and "Specimen drawer" and `16-specimen/index.html` (github.com/r3al1tym/tenfold) (the bar for craft, studied closely), and `kit/README.md` with `kit/kit.js` (the shared cabinet). Look at `concept-frames/frame5.jpg` for the plate layout's feel: take the idea and the quiet, never its details (it is an AI concept frame, and a blind reader caught its construction grid not following the shell).

## The idea, for your drawer
Your animal's mathematics is drawn in pencil on the card, and the animal grows out of that drawing and lies on it. The drawing must register exactly with the specimen: the drawn spiral is the spiral the chambers follow, and the numbered points are the points the cells grow from. At the hero frame the construction still shows where the animal does not cover it, so a viewer can check the rule against the creature. Along the foot of the card a row of three to five small pencil trials shows the same rule at other parameter values, generated by the same code as the animal, each with its value lettered under it and the real one ticked. One hand-lettered ink label states the plate number, the binomial (italic) with its author and year, the common name, and the rule in one line.

## The page contract (same as every tenfold page)
- One self-contained HTML file drawing on Canvas 2D: no images, fonts, libraries or network.
- Inline `kit.js` verbatim between its marker lines, in the first `<script>`; your drawer's code follows. Run `node kit/sync.mjs <your index.html>` to (re)inline the current kit. Never edit the kit text inside your page: if the kit lacks something, write it in your own code, and say so in your report.
- Slow surfaces (the animal's textures, simulations, the card, the frame) are baked once at load into canvases or float buffers. `seek(t)` composes frame t from them.
- Seeded randomness only: `rngFor('stable:key')`, `nzc('key')`. Never `Math.random`, never time-dependent state. `seek(t)` must draw the same frame for the same t regardless of what was drawn before.
- `kitStart(init, seek)` gives you `window.__riso`, `?t=2.5` and `?play`.
- Timing: DUR = 6 s. The build finishes at about 4.5 s (BUILD_END) and holds the hero at 5.2 s (HERO). Only the glass breathes after the build.
- Budget: init under about 10 s and each seek under about 1.5 s on this machine (the render tool prints both). A film will be shot from these pages later.

## One cabinet, one light
- Camera straight above; one window at the upper left; shadows fall down-right with offset height × K and softness growing with height (use `castShadow`). Glass lid, dust and window falloff come from the kit and go on top of everything.
- Real scale: MM = 3.6 px per mm across the drawer (the floor is about 264 × 330 mm). Size the specimen at its true size where it fits, or say on the label that it is a juvenile or a fragment; never silently scale an animal up.
- The drawer floor is the kit's card (white, or black for the web). The specimen lies on it, raised where it is thick, with true shadows.
- Pencil is the curator's construction: thin, exact, quiet. Ink is only the label. The animal is the only coloured, physical thing in the drawer, and spends all the boldness.

## The build
The glass catches the window first (kit). The construction draws itself in pencil, line by line, at a draughtsman's pace. Then the animal grows out of it by the same rule: your animal's one designed moment, given in your card. The trial row and the label are lettered near the end. Build in whole, eased motions with weight; nothing flies in, nothing scatters, no particles, no glow.

## True
Every number on the card is computed by your code or comes from the literature, and the label and README text you suggest state it plainly. If a model is a proposal rather than settled science, say "proposed" (for example, the honeycomb's wax-flow mechanism). Anatomy is real: count the scutes, the septa, the radials, the columns. A blind reader will check the tick against the trials, so the trials must come from the same model as the animal.

## Craft: what the review will judge
Fresh art-director reviewers will see only your 5.2 s hero frame at feed size (540 × 675) beside `16-specimen`'s hero, and judge it as the bar. They will be looking for: no AI-generic look; a real construction a viewer can see; agency-grade finish at the scale of a scale, a tubercle, a thread; one bold element (the animal) in a quiet drawer. They will flag flat vector fills, noise used as a cover for missing structure, construction lines that do not register, fake numbers, wrong anatomy, cramped or empty composition, and type that is too small or too loud.

## How to check your work
- Render: `node tools/render.mjs NN-name/index.html /tmp/ma/NN 1.5,3.0,4.5,5.2` writes `tN.png` (full size) and `tN-small.png` (540 wide), and prints init and seek times, page errors, whether the page source contains `Math.random`, and a determinism check.
- View only the small PNGs, or crops you cut with sharp (`cd tools && node -e "require('sharp')('in.png').extract({left:0,top:0,width:500,height:400}).toFile('out.png')"`). A full-size PNG read will exhaust your context.
- Determinism in headless Chrome: drawing a small canvas scaled up straight onto the frame (a quarter-resolution blurred shadow drawn at W×H, say) can make pixels differ by a few levels between seeks. Upscale into a full-size offscreen canvas first and draw that unscaled (found by the urchin builder).
- Look at your own renders often: the hero at feed size for composition, 100 % crops for craft, and the four build times for the moment. Keep going until you would set your hero beside the moths without apology.

## Scope and reporting
- Work only in your own folder. Do not commit, push or touch other folders; the lead commits.
- Write a short `NOTES.md` in your folder: the mathematics and how the page builds it, in two or three plain paragraphs in the register of the README's style paragraphs (what the mathematics is and how the page builds it, with real numbers), plus the label text and the trial values.
- Finish with a brief report: what you built, init and seek times, and what you think is still weak.
