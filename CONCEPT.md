# Math-Animals: concept

**Pick: ten drawers from one cabinet. In each, an animal's mathematics is drawn in pencil on the card, and the animal grows out of that drawing and is left lying on it. Along the foot of the card, the same rule at other values is sketched in pencil and never built; the real value is ticked.**

The moths in `16-specimen` are honest constructions that keep their mathematics hidden: a wing is a polar table and a pattern is an iso-line of radius, and only the code says so. Math-Animals moves the construction onto the card. Each drawer opens empty, the curator's construction is drawn in graphite (the spiral, the lattice, the seed points), and the animal is then built by that construction over 4.5 seconds. At the hero frame the drawing still runs out from under the specimen, so a viewer sees the rule and the creature at once. The trials along the foot show that the animal is one setting of its rule: the other settings make animals that do not exist.

## Card

- **Person.** Someone who has seen tenfold, design-literate, meeting the series first as a contact sheet in a feed and then opening one drawer in a browser. They know what a nautilus looks like and have heard that it is "a golden spiral".
- **Feeling.** Wonder that holds up under scrutiny. The arc runs from "a museum drawer", to "that pencil drawing is a rule", to "the animal is the rule", and ends on the plain fact that the rule is the real one.
- **Wow moment.** The handover in each build, around 1.5 to 3.5 s, when the pencil construction turns into the animal: the drawn spiral fills with chambers, the numbered points become scales, the seed dots become wing cells. Each animal composes its own version of it (listed below).
- **Future.** Generative form that is true to nature as well as pretty: in a world of images made by models, these are pictures you can check line by line.
- **Reference to beat.** `16-specimen` on craft: physical light, scale-level detail, a build with weight. D'Arcy Thompson's *On Growth and Form* on honesty: every construction is the real one. Haeckel's *Kunstformen der Natur* on density and how much there is to look at. The series has to match the moths on craft and beat Thompson on the visual pull of the construction.
- **Studio question.** A studio like Pentagram or Universal Everything would make the rule visible and keep it true, then let the animal take the light.
- **Concept line.** *Each animal grows out of its own construction drawing.*

## Frames considered

Eight seeds, two per lens. Four were rendered as concept frames (`concept-frames/`, GPT Image 2.5).

- Borrowed artifact: (a) a museum drawer whose typed label gives the equation the way a label gives a locality [frame 1]; (b) a folio plate with an engraved construction figure beside a coloured animal. Cut (b): it is the reflex for "maths and nature", and tenfold already has two paper pages.
- Material behaviour: (c) the build is the animal's own growth, so page time is growth time; (d) the construction drawn in pencil on the tray card, with the specimen laid exactly on it [frame 4].
- The datum as hero: (e) the rule's parameters typed on the label (folded into a); (f) a morphospace drawer, with the real animal in the centre tray among the "impossible" animals the same rule makes at other parameters [frame 2].
- Scale or time shift: (g) a growth series, hatchling to adult, laid in a row so time becomes space [frame 3]; (h) a microscope slide. Cut (h): it pulls toward protists and glow.

A blind reader graded the four frames against the seven bar words; none passed all seven. It ranked the ideas 2, 4, 1, 3. Frame 2 (the morphospace) carried the deepest idea, the real animal as one choice among possible ones, but spread the interest over nine equal cells with no single moment. Frame 4 (the pangolin completing a drawn spiral) had the one moment of wonder but invented its numbers. Frame 1 is the field's oldest image, and frame 3 shows no mathematics. The reader also flagged the oak vitrine as a stock cabinet-of-curiosities look. The drawer stays anyway, because the brief extends the approved `16-specimen` page, and the construction is what makes these drawers distinct.

The next round combined frames 2 and 4 (frames 5 and 6). The animal lies on its construction, and a row of small pencil trials along the foot of the card shows the rule at other values, with the real one ticked. Frame 5 (the cone shell over its growth-line grid, five pattern trials beneath) reads as one quiet museum plate with a single bold element, and it sets the layout. A second blind read passed it on five of the seven words. It failed Craft because the drawn grid does not follow the shell, and Distinct on the drawer; it also asked for one true, checkable claim per page, trials generated by the real model, and no spiral repeated as the main idea. So the pick is (d) + (c) + (a) + (f): the pencil construction is the series device, the build is the growth, the label states the rule, and the morphospace survives as the trial row, drawn and never built. The ten pages must fix what the frames got wrong: every number is computed, every anatomy is real, and each page has one moment.

## The phrase

The series stands on the mathematics alone. The moths carry words because that page's job was the phrase. Here the job is that every mark is made by the rule, and a word set into a Turing pattern or a Voronoi wing would be the one mark on the page the rule did not make. The phrase is still in the series, in the trial rows: each drawer holds one real animal and sketches of the impossible ones its rule also allows. The phrase itself appears once, as the dedication on the cabinet's index card.

## One visual system

- One cabinet. Every page is a drawer of the same quartersawn oak with a glazed lid, seen from straight above at 1080×1350, lit by one window at the upper left, so shadows fall down and to the right. This is the light, glass and dust of `16-specimen`.
- Card. The drawer floor is a card mount (white for most specimens, black for the spider's web, as real web collectors use). The pencil construction is drawn on it in graphite (white pencil on the black card).
- The label. Each drawer has one small card label, hand-lettered in India ink in a single curator's hand: plate number, binomial, common name, and the rule in one line. A shared single-stroke font draws it, and the same font letters the pencil annotations.
- The plate layout. The animal and its construction take the card's upper four-fifths. The foot holds the trial row (three to five small pencil sketches of the rule at other values, each with its value, the real one ticked) and the label. Each builder composes the animal freely inside that layout.
- The build grammar. The glass catches the window, the construction draws itself, the animal grows from it, the trials and the label are lettered, and the build ends at about 4.5 s with a hero frame at 5.2 s.
- The kit. `kit/kit.js` holds the shared drawer, card, light, glass, shadows, graphite, ink hand, label and page contract, and every page inlines it verbatim between marker lines, so the ten drawers stay one cabinet.

## The ten

1. **Chambered nautilus** (*Nautilus pompilius*), the logarithmic spiral r = a·e^(bθ). The shell grows by gnomons: each new chamber is the last one scaled by a constant ratio, so the shape never changes as it grows. Moment: the chambers seal one after another outward along the drawn spiral, each septum a scaled copy of the last, until the open living chamber flares and the cut nacre takes the light. Trials: the whorl expansion rate W = e^{2πb} at 2, 3 (ticked) and 5, from crowded whorls to a shell that uncoils.
2. **Honeybee and comb** (*Apis mellifera*), the honeycomb theorem: of all ways to cut a plane into equal cells, hexagons use the least wall (Hales, 1999). The bees build round cells, and the warm wax flows until the circles become rounded hexagons (Karihaloo et al., 2013). Moment: a close-packed field of wax circles relaxes into hexagons, wall by wall, before the bees settle on the comb. Trials: the three regular tilings with their wall per unit area, triangles 4.559, squares 4.000, hexagons 3.722 (ticked).
3. **Garden orb-weaver and its web** (*Araneus diadematus*), mounted on black card. The capture thread is an Archimedean spiral r = a + bθ laid across near-equal radials, and its glue coat breaks into droplets by the Rayleigh–Plateau instability. Moment: once the spiral is laid, the glue beads up along every turn at once at the instability's wavelength, and the beads take the light like dew. Trials: the spiral spacing as concentric rings, logarithmic and Archimedean (ticked).
4. **Sea urchin test** (*Echinus esculentus*), five-fold dihedral symmetry D5. Plates are added at the apical disc and pushed down ten meridional columns, tubercles packed on each plate. Moment: one sector is built, and the other four are its rotations through 72°; the symmetry closes when the fifth sector meets the first. Trials: four-, five- (ticked), six- and seven-fold tests, all impossible except one.
5. **Dragonfly** (*Anax imperator*), pinned. Between the main veins the wing cells are a Voronoi tessellation of seed points (Hoffmann et al., 2018). Moment: the seeds scatter between the drawn main veins and the cells crystallise as their Voronoi edges, then the membrane takes its sheen and the pterostigma darkens. Trials: cells from a square grid, a hexagonal lattice, and seeds scattered at a minimum spacing (ticked).
6. **Radiated tortoise carapace** (*Astrochelys radiata*), growth by accretion. Each scute grows ring by ring from its areola, so its growth annuli are offset curves of the hatchling scute, and the yellow rays run along the distance field's gradient. Moment: the hatchling shell is drawn, then every scute adds rings at once, and the stars lengthen with them. Trials: a hatchling scaled up whole (no rings, impossible for a shell that grows at its edges) against growth by accretion (ticked).
7. **Sea fan** (*Gorgonia ventalina*), branching by space colonisation. Tips grow toward free space marked by attractor points and fuse where they meet, closing the fan into a net. Moment: branches climb from the holdfast through the pencil dots, consuming them, and tips that meet fuse until the mesh closes. Trials: a branching tree that never fuses, the fused net (ticked), and a net so dense it closes into a blade.
8. **Venus' flower basket** (*Euplectella aspergillum*), a glass sponge. Its skeleton is a square lattice on a cylinder, braced on the diagonals in a checkerboard of alternate cells, wound with ridges at 45°. A pair of shrimp lives inside. Moment: the square lattice is laid, then the diagonals brace every other cell, then the helical ridges wind on. Trials: the four lattices Fernandes et al. (2021) tested, unbraced, single-diagonal, fully braced, and the sponge's double diagonals on alternate cells (ticked).
9. **Cloth-of-gold cone** (*Conus textile*), a one-dimensional activator–inhibitor system along the growing lip (Meinhardt). The shell's surface is the system's space-time diagram, with each growth line one time step. Moment: the lip sweeps round the whorl writing the pattern row by row, and the triangles close where two waves meet. Trials: other activator–inhibitor settings on the same outline: stripes, dots, triangles (ticked), zigzags, blank.
10. **Semicircle angelfish, juvenile** (*Pomacanthus semicirculatus*), Turing's reaction–diffusion on a growing domain. As the fish grows, its stripes keep their spacing, so new stripes insert between old ones, as Kondo and Asai predicted and then watched happen in this species in 1995. Moment: the fish grows on its drawn outlines and a new white stripe splits in between two old ones. Trials: the same reaction at other ratios of diffusion rates: spots, stripes (ticked), labyrinth.

## Left out, and why

Fourier epicycles draw an outline but do not shape any animal, and epicycles drawing a picture is a well-worn demo. No animal carries a Penrose tiling. Lissajous figures describe a hummingbird's wingtip path rather than its form. A giraffe's Voronoi coat would not fit in a drawer, so the dragonfly's wing carries Voronoi instead. The pangolin was in the first list for golden-angle phyllotaxis, but a blind read of its frame called the claim weak (it holds for pinecones, not pangolins), and a curled pangolin reads as a pinecone, so the honeybee took its place with a claim a reader can check on the page.
